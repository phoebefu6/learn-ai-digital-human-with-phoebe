Learn AI Digital Human with Phoebe

Six sessions, 45 minutes each, and one decision you can defend in a room. Most courses on this subject start with how to make a synthetic presenter. This one starts with whether you should, because that is what you will be answerable for. Along the way: what each vendor genuinely requires, why the on-screen face is the weakest lever in multimedia learning, and what happens to every video already made when the person in them leaves.

The six sessions 🎭 · one artifact, built across all of them

Bring a real decision. A presenter you are considering, a colleague who has been asked, or a policy you have been told to write. By session 6 you have a written position you can defend.

⚖️ Session 1 · easy

Should you at all

What consent each vendor actually requires, what enterprise money exempts you from, and why a meta-analysis of 86,000 people says nobody can tell.

▶ Start here ✍️ Session 2 · easy

the contract 🎭 Session 3 · medium

Making one that works

Footage requirements, prosody, and the four craft levers that take believability from 24 to 84. Then the point where craft stops helping.

hands-on 📊 Session 4 · medium

What the evidence says

The on-screen face scores d = 0.22 while the script register scores 1.11. Engagement falls with an AI instructor and measured learning does not.

uncomfortable 🚨 Session 5 · hands-on

When it is used against you

Arup lost 25 million dollars. LastPass lost nothing and published why. Three seconds of audio is enough to clone a voice.

defence 📋 Session 6 · hands-on

The policy you have to write

Article 50 puts a duty on you as deployer. Four questions no vendor answers, which means your organisation has to.

What is real here 🧾

A course about synthetic media owes you an unusually precise account of its own claims.

ClaimStatus
